# Lumen Field Analytics managed-service order LF-204

Synthetic agreement for evaluation of document retrieval. Northstar Labs purchases telemetry analytics from the fictional Lumen Field Analytics service. The order begins on 15 November 2026. The order, its operating schedules, the four incorporated Northstar standards and the final negotiated rider form one evidence package. This example does not contain real business terms, signatures or customer data.

## Order particulars and covered workloads

The purchased service has two workloads: Live Fleet receives operational device messages, while Research Bench stores synthetic experiments. Customer employee contact records used for incident coordination are part of Live Fleet's service boundary. Research Bench contains no production contact records. A provision referring to one workload must not be applied to the other merely because the same supplier operates both. The operations catalogue identifies the data stores, queues and export paths used by each workload.

The negotiated rider has priority over an inconsistent term in the operating schedules, but only for its stated workload and subject. The Northstar supplier standards are incorporated as a baseline. Where a stricter applicable standard has not been expressly changed, the parties retain it. Silence in a service-level table does not establish a waiver of a security obligation. An unsigned project note is not part of the negotiated rider.

The customer assigns an owner to the device population and validates the purpose of each imported field. Lumen checks service credentials and tenant routing before messages enter a processing queue. The customer remains responsible for whether a sensor was deployed in an appropriate business location. A demonstration dashboard does not certify the accuracy of every message supplied by a device. Corrections and rejected records are traceable to the originating import batch.

## Operating schedule A: access approval

Interactive administrative access to Live Fleet requires a phishing-resistant second factor. The identity team records the person, approved role and intended duration. Support elevation follows a named incident or maintenance task. Standing access to unrelated customer tenants is not granted as a convenience for a shared support queue. The access register distinguishes a requested role from an effective entitlement and preserves the approval history after revocation.

Device ingestion certificates are outside the interactive second-factor requirement, but each certificate is tenant-scoped and must be replaced before its 90-day expiry. A certificate cannot be reused as an operator login. The onboarding procedure checks the device identifier and assigned tenant before accepting messages. Revoking a device is independent of disabling a human employee account. Operations can present a redacted certificate-lifecycle record without exposing a private key.

Periodic access reviews focus on effective rights and business justification. They supplement rather than delay action when an employee changes responsibilities. An emergency role requires a recorded reason and retrospective review. The supplier reports recurring emergency use as a control concern rather than treating repeated approvals as proof that the normal process is unnecessary. Customer administrators manage their own ordinary dashboard users under Northstar's internal policies.

## Operating schedule B: retention and recovery

The general Live Fleet schedule removes customer telemetry 30 days after termination of the subscription. An authenticated early removal instruction starts the same workflow for the named dataset. The supplier separates the ordinary service store from corporate transaction records. A chargeback investigation cannot silently retain all telemetry because a billing account remains open. The receipt describes what was removed and what was held under a specific preservation instruction.

The baseline recovery objective for Live Fleet is four hours after the incident commander declares a recovery event. The recovery process includes restoring state, replaying accepted messages and checking queue progress. Restoring an empty dashboard is not completion. Customer validation confirms that the agreed reference population appears and that message timestamps are interpreted correctly. The event timeline records intermediate milestones without treating each milestone as a separate restart of the target.

Research Bench recovery is best effort and does not inherit the Live Fleet four-hour objective. Research samples may be reconstructed from the documented experiment definition. The service catalogue labels those experiments separately so that a successful bench demonstration is not submitted as evidence of production recovery. Any future movement of real customer records into the bench requires a revised assessment and service schedule.

## Operating schedule C: hosted data and locations

Live Fleet primary storage, computation and managed recovery storage operate in Finland and Sweden. The approved architecture diagram names the relevant component classes and the provider responsible for each. Locations of sales personnel do not define service processing locations. A move to another region is subject to the change route and privacy review, including the new operational access boundary. Replication is described explicitly rather than hidden in a general availability claim.

An Australian incident specialist may view limited Live Fleet diagnostic records through a recorded, customer-approved session. That access is separate from primary hosting and does not mean the production store has relocated to Australia. The session is scoped to the affected tenant and purpose. A proposed broader access route requires review even if the specialist's employer is already listed as a supplier. Northstar can inspect the authorization and session summary as part of its review.

Stored Live Fleet payloads, operational logs and recovery snapshots are encrypted at rest. Key-administration roles are separated from routine analytics support. A component exception must identify the affected records, responsible owner and compensating controls; it does not silently amend the coverage statement. The supplier can provide configuration samples through a controlled assurance route. Cryptographic implementation quality is not established merely by quoting a product name.

Cached troubleshooting bundles containing Live Fleet records remain subject to the same storage protection until their verified removal. They are not exempt because they are temporary or compressed. The task record identifies who created a bundle and who may access it. A bundle containing only synthetic Research Bench data is recorded as such and must not be presented as a sample proving protection of production records.

## Operating schedule D: notification and ongoing updates

Lumen's original operating schedule allows 24 hours for the first notice of an incident affecting Live Fleet. The response route accepts alerts from customers, operators and infrastructure dependencies. Initial communication may describe a suspected impact and indicate what is still being investigated. The supplier must not report an uncertainty as a confirmed fact merely to fill a template. The final rider below changes one aspect of this schedule.

After the first Live Fleet incident notice, Lumen provides a status update at least every eight hours until containment. The update identifies material changes, outstanding decisions and the next communication route. Absence of new facts can be stated explicitly. A technical recovery milestone does not necessarily mean that containment has been established. Customer questions are linked to the same incident record rather than creating contradictory parallel timelines.

Once containment is confirmed, the update interval may be extended to one working day with the customer's recorded agreement. That agreement does not change the initial-notice deadline or excuse a material-impact update. The response lead preserves the time at which containment was declared and the reason for changing the cadence. A scheduled root-cause meeting is an additional review event, not a substitute for incident updates.

## Operating schedule E: evidence, suppliers and change records

The available independent report covers Live Fleet's application tier and managed infrastructure for the first half of 2026. The evidence register names the report, issuer and review boundary. Northstar can request an authorized copy and inspect its exclusions. Lumen provides interim operating declarations for gaps, but it cannot relabel its own assurance summary as an independent report. A certification logo on a presentation has no broader scope than the supporting document.

The report does not cover the mobile field gateway added in July 2026; its inclusion is planned for the next assessment cycle. The gateway remains part of the operational asset inventory, so an inventory listing alone does not resolve the assurance gap. The parties track the review action and any compensating evidence separately. An assurance reviewer must distinguish a target completion date from an assessor's issued report.

Lumen maintains a register of delivery partners with service roles and data access. Advance-change notices identify what activity will change rather than listing only a corporate name. A supplier with no access to customer records may still be operationally important, and the service owner considers that dependency in continuity planning. The register is reviewed after a material acquisition. The negotiated rider records the customer-specific notice period and emergency exception.

## Commercial administration, deliverables and acceptance

The service charge covers the agreed message volume band and named support route. Additional implementation work requires an accepted work order describing deliverables and assumptions. A forecast of increased message volume is not approval to ingest a different category of personal data. Billing reports summarize quantities without copying raw device payloads into a finance mailbox. The parties reconcile disputed quantities using the agreed metering record.

Implementation acceptance uses a documented synthetic fleet with expected outputs and rejection cases. The customer validates tenant separation, message ordering and the field map before authorizing production ingestion. Acceptance does not certify that every real device sends accurate measurements. A defect discovered later enters the correction process with its affected scope. The release record connects the defect, correction and verification, including any temporary restriction on use.

On exit Lumen provides a documented export of the Live Fleet dataset and a record of unsupported fields. Northstar tests the package in its receiving process and identifies gaps before closing the handover. An export made for a demonstration tenant is not completion of the production exit obligation. The parties coordinate transition access without granting indefinite access to the original service. Removal and export delivery are reported as separate activities.

## Negotiated rider LF-R3: signed exceptions and substitutions

This rider changes only the explicitly named Live Fleet obligations. Research Bench retains its separate synthetic-data and best-effort operating boundary. Inconsistent generic Northstar defaults continue to be visible in the incorporated annexes for traceability, but the substitutions here take priority for Live Fleet. No other obligation is released by implication.

LF-R3 replaces the general 30-day removal window with 45 days for Live Fleet telemetry after subscription termination. The extension is specific to telemetry and does not apply to incident-contact records, which remain subject to the 30-day default. Technically isolated snapshots expire within 75 days and are unavailable for ordinary analytics. If a restore brings back previously removed records, the removal instructions are reapplied before the tenant is reopened.

LF-R3 reduces the initial Live Fleet incident-notice deadline from 24 hours to six hours after credible evidence of customer impact reaches the response lead. The clock does not wait for root-cause confirmation or a final incident classification. Existing eight-hour containment updates remain in effect and must not be confused with the initial six-hour notice. A delayed customer response does not restart either clock.

Lumen must give 35 calendar days of advance notice before a new partner gains access to Live Fleet customer records. An urgent replacement needed to restore an interrupted service may start immediately with notice within one working day. The urgent notice must identify the access boundary and interim safeguards; urgency does not authorize unrelated data use. The customer's objection and the supplier's response remain in the change register.
